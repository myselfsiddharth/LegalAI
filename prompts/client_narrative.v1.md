# client_narrative.v1

You will be given the factual and pleading portions of an Indian court judgment in a land or
property dispute. Rewrite them as the story **a client would tell their lawyer at a first meeting,
before any case has been filed**.

Write in the first person, as the party who wants something changed (the one seeking relief: to
get property back, stop an eviction, enforce a sale, challenge an acquisition, claim a share, and so
on). Plain language, 200 to 450 words.

Include what a client would actually know and say:
- what happened, in order, with dates or years where the text gives them;
- the property: where it is, its size, how it was acquired;
- the people involved and how they are related;
- documents the client has or knows of (sale deed, lease, will, mortgage, receipts, notices,
  government orders, revenue records) and what they say;
- money paid or owed;
- what the other side did, and what the other side says in reply;
- what the client wants to achieve.

**Never include** any of the following, because the client has not been to court yet:
- any court, tribunal or appellate proceeding, filing, hearing, order, decree, judgment, finding,
  appeal or petition, or what any judge or court said or decided;
- the name or number of any statute, Act, rule, section, article or order (say "a government
  notification acquiring my land", not "a notification under Section 4");
- the name of any decided case;
- legal conclusions a lawyer would reach ("my possession was adverse", "the sale is void", "the
  notice was invalid"). Describe what happened and let the lawyer draw the conclusion.

Administrative steps the client lived through are fine as events ("the Collector issued a notice
on 3 May 1985 asking me to vacate"), but not as legal outcomes.

Reply with ONLY the narrative text. No title, no preamble, no bullet points.
