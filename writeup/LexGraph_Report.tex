\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This is the single authoritative document for the project. It supersedes and replaces the
specification (\code{PROJECT.md}), the chronological development log (\code{WORKLOG.md}) and the
nine stage-acceptance reports (\code{reports/*.md}), all of which were removed; their content is
folded in here and their history remains in git. A plain-language companion for readers without a
background in law or machine learning, \code{LexGraph\_Explained.tex}, sits in this same folder.

Two results are established. \textbf{First}, the input construction used by the published state of
the art on this task beats a facts-only input by $+0.111$ to $+0.181$ AUROC, and essentially all of
that gap is disposition language surviving the deletion of the operative order. The residue extends
roughly \textbf{128 words} past the boundary --- a figure found independently on four splits --- and
a word-count-matched window set back past it scores no better than the facts on any of them. Across
26,688 judgments the effect is significant in all eight decades from the 1950s to the 2020s. The
court's reasoning does carry real signal, but only about $+0.025$ AUROC: the apparent effect is four
to seven times its true size.

\textbf{Second}, projecting extracted facts into a symbolic legal vocabulary costs $0.097$ AUROC,
which is most of what survives extraction. This is a property of discretisation rather than of any
particular ontology: a corpus-induced vocabulary of equal granularity is statistically
indistinguishable from the hand-authored one ($P=0.846$), more labels do not help, and the cost is
concentrated precisely where the vocabulary \emph{succeeds} in naming a fact.

\textbf{Third}, §\ref{sec:v2} rebuilds the \emph{intended} architecture --- the one this project set
out to construct --- correctly, and localises where it loses information. Done properly, fact
families lose nothing (0.633 from 345 dimensions against 0.629 for the 47,354-feature text they
derive from). The element layer, by contrast, reaches 0.521 and fails its acceptance test, and three
explanations for that were tested before the right one survived: the cause is the \emph{basis}, not
the encoding. At matched granularity a data-driven basis scores 0.632 where the 66-element doctrinal
basis scores 0.524. Naming all 66 induced clusters shows why: legal-substance facts alone, procedural
facts alone and dates alone all score $\approx 0.605$, so \textbf{the outcome signal is diffuse
rather than concentrated}, a spanning basis captures it and a selective doctrinal probe cannot, and no
ontology would unlock it. §\ref{sec:can78} addresses whether the published 78\% can be beaten:
trivially yes on the leaky task, almost certainly not on the honest one.

Neither critique is new to the literature, and §\ref{sec:prior} positions both against verified
prior work. What is defensibly ours is methodological, and §\ref{sec:prior} says which parts. Three
constructive results stand alongside: statute prediction at $1.9\times$ the strongest baseline,
precedent retrieval at $7.4\times$ a popularity control, and a faithfulness-tested trace mechanism.
Every number here is read from a file under \code{experiments/}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Environment \spec{3.1}}\label{sec:env}

All model calls go to ASU's hosted endpoint (Voyager, \code{https://openai.rc.asu.edu/v1}), through
the single door in \code{src/llm/client.py}. No paid API is used anywhere.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No VPN required.} The endpoint resolved to private \code{10.139.64.x} historically;
as of 2026-09-18 it resolves to public Cloudflare addresses and answers from a residential
connection.
\item \textbf{Generation}: \code{llama4-scout-17b} by default. \textbf{Embeddings}:
\code{qwen3-embedding-8b} (4096-dim). Only three of the roughly forty-nine advertised models serve
\code{/v1/embeddings}; the rest 404 there, and the model list carries no capability metadata, so
probing is the only way to tell an embedder from a generator.
\item \textbf{Content-addressed cache}, keyed \code{sha256(model, messages, params)} in SQLite.
Re-running an experiment is free --- the LLM baseline ran at a 100\% hit rate over 600 calls. The
cache holds 162,230 entries.
\item \textbf{\code{ok=False} means ``never got an answer''}, which is deliberately distinct from a
model that replied with nothing. Conflating the two turned a network outage into a finding twice
before this was enforced (§\ref{sec:engineering}).
\item \textbf{90-second timeout.} The client default of 600\,s $\times$ 3 retries froze an early
batch for over twenty minutes with no output.
\item \textbf{Parallelism}: 96 workers for extraction, 24 for embeddings; throughput went from 10.3
to 38.6 documents per second.
\end{itemize}

Setup: \code{.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt}, then \code{.env} at the project root with
\code{VOYAGER\_API\_KEY=<key>} from \code{voyager.rc.asu.edu} $\rightarrow$ LLM Access $\rightarrow$
Create Key.

\section{Corpus \spec{4}}\label{sec:corpus}

\textbf{26,688} Indian Supreme Court judgment PDFs, 1950--2025, 6.8\,GB, held locally in
\code{Data/raw\_pdfs/<year>/}. A provided spreadsheet identifies \textbf{6,954} of them as land or
property disputes; that subset is the working corpus for §\ref{sec:labels}--§\ref{sec:traces}, and
§\ref{sec:scale} drops the restriction and uses all 26,688.

\paragraph{Extracting the archives} Two of the four source zips contain filenames with invalid
UTF-8 bytes. APFS rejects those names, \code{unzip} misreports it as \code{write error (disk
full?)}, then blocks on a \code{Continue? (y/n)} prompt --- and on non-interactive stdin takes EOF
and \textbf{silently aborts the whole archive}, exiting 0. That is how a first attempt produced
12,422 of 26,688 files and reported success. Extraction must go through Python's \code{zipfile} with
each name sanitised (\code{encode("utf-8","replace")}, NFC-normalise, strip control characters), and
the count verified afterwards.

\paragraph{Joining spreadsheet rows to PDFs} \code{src/data/adapter.py} joins on the exact filename
the spreadsheet supplies: \textbf{6,954 of 6,954 (100\%)}, asserted at import time against
\code{MIN\_PDF\_JOIN\_RATE = 0.99} so a regression halts the pipeline instead of silently shrinking
the dataset. An earlier normalised-title join achieved 0.6\%, because judgment filenames carry a
copy marker after the year (\code{...\_on\_23\_May\_1957\_1}) and titles do not --- so stripping
``trailing digits'' from both removes the \emph{year} from every title and every pair mismatches by
exactly that year.

\paragraph{Text} 6,954 of 6,954 extracted, zero failures, zero near-empty. Median 5,302 words.

\paragraph{What the dataset readme claims and the PDFs do not have} The readme describes structured
sections. Sampled across six decades, \code{FACTS} appears as a header in \textbf{0} of 12
judgments, \code{ISSUE} in 2, \code{ARGUMENT} in 2, \code{REASONING} in 0. There is nothing to
segment on, which is why §\ref{sec:masking} classifies units by cue rather than by heading.

\paragraph{Citation metadata} The provided ``precedents'' lists mix genuine case-to-case citations
with statute and article references; only about \textbf{32.5\%} are real case citations. The
classifier output used downstream is
\code{Data/processed/citations\_classified.jsonl}. The metadata is Indian Kanoon's link graph and is
independently scraped, not ground truth --- English and Privy Council authorities are systematically
absent. Separately, only \textbf{11,991 of 46,904} case-citation edges (25.6\%) point at a case that
is in this corpus at all, because the corpus is Supreme Court only while judgments cite High Courts,
the Privy Council and English decisions. Recall must therefore be reported against \emph{reachable}
authorities with the raw figure beside it.

\section{Labels \spec{5.1}}\label{sec:labels}

Two independent labellers, made to agree.

\textbf{Rules} (\code{src/data/labels.py}) locate the operative order by regular expression and
classify it. The hard part is that Indian orders dispose of several proceedings in one breath ---
``the appeal is allowed \ldots\ and the writ petition stands dismissed'' --- where the second clause
is a \emph{consequence} of the first and a naive matcher reads it as the outcome. Proceedings are
therefore ranked into tiers: an appeal or special leave petition (\code{\_PROC\_T1}) governs, and a
writ petition, suit, revision or application (\code{\_PROC\_T2}) is consulted only when tier 1 is
silent. That change alone cut rules-versus-LLM conflicts from 196 to 107. A bounded gap
(\code{\_GAP}) forbids a proceeding noun from binding across another disposition verb, and a negator
check rejects ``we do not think it proper to again remand the matter''.

\textbf{LLM} sees the same order window and must quote the span it relied on.

\textbf{Agreement: 87.0\%} on the 4,317 cases where both decided and the quote verified. Conflicts
are held as \code{UNRESOLVED} and queued in \code{Data/interim/label\_conflicts.jsonl} rather than
resolved by trusting one labeller.

\section{Masking \spec{5.2}}\label{sec:masking}

\code{src/data/mask.py} keeps only material that existed before the court decided. Units are
classified by cue, not by heading, because there are no headings (§\ref{sec:corpus}).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Section} & \textbf{Kept} & \textbf{Why} \\
\midrule
Caption, parties, judges & no & Judge identity predicts but is not a legal signal; party names let a model memorise repeat litigants. \\
Facts & \textbf{yes} & The genuine input. \\
Procedural history & \emph{isolated} & Legitimately known in advance but a shortcut, so it goes to its own channel \code{prior\_court\_text} and feature group \code{Q}, never mixed in silently. \\
Arguments of counsel & \textbf{yes} & Written by the parties, before the decision. \\
Court's reasoning & no & Written after, knowing the answer. \\
Operative order & no & This is the label. \\
HEADNOTE (pre-2000) & no & An editorial summary that states outcome and authorities outright. \\
Page furniture & no & Indian Kanoon URLs, page numbers, running headers. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Within kept units there is a second, sentence-level scrub: sentences matching \code{OUTCOME\_CUE}
are dropped, and sentences matching \code{PRIOR\_COURT\_DISPOSITION} are diverted to the shortcut
channel. Average retained length is about 2,500 words of the original 5,300.

Three variants are produced per case, which is what makes §\ref{sec:leakage} possible:
\code{masked\_text} (facts and pleadings only), \code{ildc\_style\_text} (everything but the order)
and \code{ildc\_style\_no\_headnote}.

\paragraph{The sensitivity control, and why everything else depends on it} A claim to have removed
leakage is untestable on its own. So a deliberately leaky arm is built --- a model trained on
\emph{only} the deleted text --- and must score near-perfectly. If it does not, the order detector is
broken and the masked arm cannot be trusted either.

\section{Splits \spec{5.3}}\label{sec:splits}

A random split fails here twice over. The same dispute generates several judgments (appeal, review,
connected matter), so groups must not be split; and training on later cases to predict earlier ones
is time travel.

\begin{itemize}
\item \code{forum\_heldout} --- three forums of origin held out entirely. Train 3,350 / dev 455 /
test 902. Hash \code{6e346dd75b7e7d84}.
\item \code{temporal\_2004\_2013} --- train $\leq$2004, dev 2004--2013, test $>$2013. Train 3,446 /
dev 480 / test 768. Hash \code{43d9d645667ad3a5}.
\end{itemize}

Every results file records the split hash, so a number traces to the exact split that produced it.

\paragraph{A conflict a unit test caught} Grouping by a case group's \emph{maximum} year put 1970
cases in ``test $>$2013''; by \emph{minimum} year it put 2025 cases in ``train $\leq$2004''. Both are
time travel, and no assignment satisfies both constraints for a group straddling the boundary. The
13 straddling groups are dropped and the count recorded. \code{tests/test\_stage1.py} enforces it.

\section{Fact extraction \spec{6.1}}\label{sec:extraction}

A model reads each segmented unit and proposes \emph{facts}: atomic assertions, each carrying a
verbatim quote, who asserted it, and whether it was disputed. \code{src/grounding.py} locates the
quote in the source; a fact whose quote cannot be found is \textbf{discarded, not corrected}.

\textbf{Grounding rate 0.92.} Current scale: \textbf{96,623 facts across 4,702 cases}, median 17 per
case, 15,377 claim records.

\paragraph{Segmentation} The first implementation split on blank lines and returned \emph{one unit}
for 57.7\% of cases, because this PDF text frequently contains no blank lines. A sentence-window
tier was added: median units per case 1 $\rightarrow$ 47, unusable cases 57.7\% $\rightarrow$ 2.6\%.

\paragraph{The verifier was the bug, not the extractor} An early version rejected 42\% of quotes
that were in fact correct: PDF extraction inserts hyphenation artifacts (\code{plain- tiff's}), and a
20-character floor rejected true short quotes such as ``Appeal dismissed.'' (15 characters). After
repairing hyphenation and lowering the floor, the verified rate on a hand-checked set went from
58.3\% to 91.7\%, with 15 of 16 confirmed present by eye.

\paragraph{Model selection} Six models on an identical 20-case set:

\paragraph{Attribution, and an unexplained ceiling} Party attribution sits at \textbf{0.19--0.20}.

to a party has not moved. A prediction that recovering wrongly-deleted argument text would fix it
was tested and refuted (§\ref{sec:retracted}). The cause is unknown.

\section{Canonical vocabulary \spec{6.2}}\label{sec:vocab}

Each fact is mapped from free text to a controlled label drawn from a 345-entry vocabulary generated
from the ontology's named fields. \code{ontology/ontology\_v1.yaml} defines 15 claims, 66 elements, 6
defence families and 4 remedy tiers.

The appeal of a controlled vocabulary is comparability across cases: you can count element
satisfaction, mine patterns, and reason symbolically. The cost is that two distinct facts mapping to
one label become indistinguishable --- and §\ref{sec:discretisation} shows that cost is severe.

\paragraph{Three failures worth not repeating} (i) The canonicaliser's self-reported ``NEW'' rate of
0.7\% was untrustworthy: 16.7\% of facts were out of vocabulary and unflagged. Out-of-vocabulary is
now decided by checking the answer, not the flag; at scale the model flags only 75 of 3,155 (2.4\%).
(ii) Batching with a shared union menu wrecked label fidelity, and out-of-vocabulary rose with menu
size (51\%/75\%/76\% at batch 4/8/16); per-fact menus restored parity (32.3\% vs 34.4\% unbatched).
(iii) Repointing \code{PROMPT\_ID} left \code{canonicalize\_one} validating a batched reply against a
single-object schema, so it returned \code{None} for everything --- and it is the \emph{fallback}
path. Split into \code{PROMPT\_ID\_ONE} and \code{PROMPT\_ID}.

\textbf{The taxonomy is not supported by the data}, and is reported as such rather than presented as

\section{Statutes \spec{9.1, 9.2}}\label{sec:statutes}

\code{src/authorities/normalize.py} reduces citations to canonical \code{CODE:provision} form over a
registry of 42 Acts. Three properties of real Indian citations forced the design:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Act names arrive truncated.} The most frequent ``Act'' in 1,200 sampled judgments is
\emph{Property Act} (644 mentions) --- the \emph{Transfer of Property Act} with its head cut off by a
line break. \emph{India Act} (516) is the \emph{Government of India Act}. Truncations resolve through
a path tagged \code{resolved\_by="truncated"}, so a guess is never reported as a full-name match.
\item \textbf{Generic back-references dominate}: \emph{the Act}, \emph{the said Act} (311+), which
resolve only by looking back to the last Act actually named, within a bounded window.
\item \textbf{Implicit Acts}: a bare \emph{Article} means the Constitution; an \emph{Order} or
\emph{Rule} means the Civil Procedure Code.
\end{itemize}

Coverage over 1,500 judgments: 58,295 citations, \textbf{61.4\% resolved}, median 6 distinct
provisions per case, 149 cases with none.

\paragraph{A copy control killed the first result} Predicting the provisions that apply reached
micro-F1 0.447 and looked strong. A system that does nothing but copy provisions already named
verbatim in the input scores \textbf{0.713} --- far higher. The ``prediction'' was largely string
matching. The honest version removes every provision the input mentions, leaving only those that
must be inferred:

system responds to the facts rather than exploiting citation skew.

Two sub-results: canonical atom overlap (0.021) falls \emph{below} the popularity control, so the
symbolic representation fails here too; and max-pooling made the dense arm \emph{worse} (0.039 vs
0.053), a prediction that had to be withdrawn.

\subsection{Where the advantage actually comes from}\label{sec:contamination}

The final 512 tokens sit exactly where the operative order used to be. If the boundary is even
slightly imperfect, that arm --- and no other --- is contaminated. Three measurements, every arm
exactly 512 words so volume is held constant.

\paragraph{1. The setback curve} Four non-overlapping windows stepping back from the end. Both
predicted shapes were written into the module before it ran: \emph{residual leakage} predicts a
cliff after the first window, because contamination cannot reach back 512 words; \emph{reasoning}
predicts gradual decay with every window above \code{masked}.

It is unambiguously the cliff (table in §\ref{sec:scale}, all four splits). \textbf{512 words of the
court's reasoning taken from anywhere but the very end carry no more outcome signal than the facts
do}, and on the corpus-wide runs slightly less.

\paragraph{2. The mechanism, visible in the text} Indian judgments announce the conclusion one
sentence \emph{before} the operative order:

\begin{quote}
``For the foregoing reasons we find no merit in the appeal. \emph{The appeal is dismissed with
costs.}''
\end{quote}

The detector marks the boundary at the second sentence, which is \textbf{correct} --- that is the
operative order. The first sentence stays, and states the outcome just as plainly.

\paragraph{3. Depth} Sweeping the window back in fine steps locates the boundary, and AUROC falls
together with the disposition-cue rate --- what a single shared cause looks like. On
\code{forum\_heldout}: offset 0 gives AUROC 0.838 at cue rate 0.419; 32 words 0.780/0.296; 64 words
0.719/0.208; \textbf{128 words 0.684/0.131, no longer significant}; 256 words 0.676/0.099; 512 words
0.669/0.089.

\paragraph{What cue-scrubbing cannot do} Deleting every cue-\emph{bearing sentence} from the final
window drops it 0.838 $\rightarrow$ 0.775; evaluating only on the 489 cases with no detected cue
gives 0.742 against \code{masked}'s 0.646 on those same cases. Both remain above \code{masked}, so
sentence-level scrubbing does \textbf{not} fully clean the window --- there is telegraphing language
the patterns miss (``we find no merit in the submission'', ``the conclusion is inescapable''). This
is exactly why the setback curve is the primary evidence: it never relies on detecting a cue.

past it scores no better than the facts on any split.

\smallskip
With the tail removed, the court's \emph{entire} reasoning adds about $\mathbf{+0.025}$ AUROC. The
apparent effect is \textbf{four to seven times} its real size. Measured on \textbf{26,688} Supreme
Court judgments, 1950--2025, every case type, significant in \textbf{all eight decades}.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{The intended architecture, built properly and measured (v2)}\label{sec:v2}

Everything above measures a pipeline that was \emph{specified} one way and \emph{implemented}
another. The intended design --- the professor's framing, and the project's original plan --- runs:

\begin{center}
facts $\rightarrow$ fact families $\rightarrow$ fact patterns by claim family $\rightarrow$
\textbf{elements} $\rightarrow$ claims and defences $\rightarrow$ issues $\rightarrow$ win/lose
\end{center}

\noindent
with a second input stream from \textbf{authorities} (statutes, precedents) supplying the rules that
define each element. §\ref{sec:discretisation} appeared to condemn that design. It did not, because
v1 got two things wrong about it, and §\ref{sec:v2} is the rebuild. The code lives in \code{v2/},
additive and importing from \code{src/} rather than duplicating it; \code{v2/ARCHITECTURE.md} is its
plan.

\paragraph{Error 1 --- step 2 was built in its lossiest form} The design says \emph{``fact family ---
classification model run on all embeddings of the facts''}. v1 instead hard-mapped each fact onto one
of 345 authored labels by \code{argmax}.

\paragraph{Error 2 --- every layer was scored on win/lose} Which §\ref{sec:outcome} shows is near
unlearnable here. The visible consequence is that v1's model runs with
\code{features\_per\_group = \{F, S, R, C, Q\}}: groups \code{P} and \code{E}, the two middle stages
of the intended design, are both \textbf{empty}. In v2 every stage is scored on its own task.

\paragraph{A correction, recorded rather than quietly fixed} The first write-up of this result claimed

remains the largest single improvement available, but it is a combination, not one mechanism.

\subsection{S4 --- the element layer}\label{sec:s4}

v1 skipped this entirely because the burden metadata it wanted does not exist for any of the 66
elements (B2, §\ref{sec:blockers}), leaving the architecture's central claim unmeasured. v2 builds it
unweighted: one call per (case, claim), a three-way verdict per element, and a \textbf{verbatim quote}
that is mechanically checked against the facts shown. A verdict whose quote cannot be located is
discarded and counted as a refusal, which yields a fabrication metric needing \emph{no ground truth}.

Full run: 4,674 cases $\times$ top-2 claims $=$ 9,348 calls, 102 minutes.
\textbf{40,078 gated verdicts} --- 13,008 SATISFIED (32\%), 1,481 NOT\_SATISFIED (3.7\%), 25,589
UNCLEAR (64\%). \textbf{Fabrication 0.62\%} (206 quotes absent, 44 too short). Zero never-answered,
zero schema failures.

\paragraph{The model had to change, and establishing why took three tests} \code{llama4-scout-17b} ---
v1's default everywhere, chosen on an extraction benchmark where it matched models 6.8$\times$ slower
--- emits \textbf{zero} NOT\_SATISFIED. A layer that cannot say that cannot express the
``or defeat'' half of the design, so this was blocking rather than cosmetic.

still gave zero). It is the model --- and the larger one fabricates \emph{less}.
\textbf{A model choice validated for extraction does not transfer to judgement tasks.}

\paragraph{The verdicts are legally sensible} The clearest NOT\_SATISFIED produced:
\emph{``Sham Lal was a tenant of a room in property unit No.\ B-VI-33''}, defeating an

\subsection{Can the published 78\% be beaten?}\label{sec:can78}

The question splits in two and only one half is worth answering.

\textbf{On the published task} --- reasoning included --- we already reach \textbf{77.6\% accuracy}
with TF-IDF and logistic regression against the reported 78\%, and a fine-tuned transformer reading
the same window would very likely clear it. \textbf{That result would be worthless}, because
§\ref{sec:contamination} shows the window is essentially all leftover disposition language. Beating
78\% that way means getting better at reading the answer off the page.

\textbf{On the honest task} --- facts and arguments only --- the best arm reaches \textbf{62.1\%
accuracy}, sixteen points below. Six independent lines say that gap is close to irreducible: the
masked-text ceiling of 0.659--0.666 across four splits and 26,688 judgments; every structured model at
or below a per-decade majority baseline; five LLM configurations likewise; model and LLM errors
statistically \emph{independent} (§\ref{sec:outcome}); all kinds of fact scoring $\approx 0.605$
(§\ref{sec:names}); and only 0.043 of headroom above S2.

\paragraph{The one legitimate gap, and it is ours} \textbf{Nothing in this project was ever
fine-tuned.} Every model here is TF-IDF with logistic regression, plus zero-shot and few-shot LLM
calls; ILDC used fine-tuned transformer models with hierarchical encoding. In this literature
fine-tuning typically buys 2--6 accuracy points over TF-IDF, so an honest estimate is
$0.62 \rightarrow 0.66$--$0.68$ --- real, worth having, and still ten points short. Three smaller
legitimate levers: the 14,168 rules-labelled cases of §\ref{sec:scale} (2.7$\times$ more training
data); cleaner labels, since 87\% labeller agreement mathematically caps measurable accuracy; and
judge or bench identity, which \emph{is} known before a decision and which §\ref{sec:masking}
excluded on principle --- though v1's metadata-only arm already reached 0.612 and adding facts to it
did not help, so it likely saturates near 0.63 too.

\begin{tcolorbox}[plainbox,title={The comparison to refuse}]
\textbf{Never place this project's honest number beside the published 78\%.} A reader sees 0.68
against 0.78 and concludes we did worse, when the two measure different things and ours is strictly
harder. That comparison is the exact error this project exists to expose.

\smallskip
State both with the gap as the result: \emph{on the published input construction we reach 77.6\%,
essentially reproducing the reported 78\%; on facts and arguments alone the same apparatus reaches
62.1\%. The fifteen-point gap is the measurement.}
\end{tcolorbox}

The experiment still worth running is therefore \textbf{not} a chase for 78\% but a fine-tuned
transformer on masked text, to make the ceiling claim defensible. A reviewer can currently object
that only TF-IDF was tried, and that objection is fair. It also cuts both ways: reaching $\approx
0.68$ strengthens the ceiling considerably, while reaching $\approx 0.75$ would show the ceiling
claim was wrong --- which is worth discovering before publication rather than after.

\subsection{What survives as ours}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The volume-controlled positional setback curve.} Every prior method located identifies
leakage \emph{lexically} or by \emph{human annotation}. Ours needs no cue detector, no annotator and
no feature flagging --- only word-count-matched windows at varying distance.
\item \textbf{Evidence that this matters, not merely differs.} Our own cue audit \textbf{failed to
replicate in three of four runs} ($+0.150$, $-0.058$, $+0.018$, $\approx 0$) while the positional
curve held in all four with the same boundary. So the claim is not ``we have a different method'' but
\emph{the method the field uses to localise leakage is unstable across case mixes, and here is a
cue-free one that is not} --- shown on 26,688 judgments with a failed lexical arm inside every run.
\item \textbf{The $\sim$128-word margin}, found four times. No located work states a distance. This is
the actionable output: a benchmark built this way needs that margin, not zero. It is a lower bound
under one baseline, not a constant --- against \code{ildc\_facts\_only} rather than \code{masked} the
substitute run put it at 512 with 128 and 256 marginal, so quote the sweep table and name the
baseline.
\item \textbf{The induced-versus-authored control.} Sadowski \& Chudziak explicitly conclude their
loss is ``\emph{not simply from vocabulary discretization alone}'' and do not compare an induced
vocabulary against a hand-authored one. We do, at $P=0.846$. \textbf{This is the gap in our favour
and the less crowded area}; it should lead the paper.
\item \textbf{A cleared baseline} (§\ref{sec:baseline}). Prior work does not verify its own clean arm
with the same instrument.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Are we ``breaking the industry''? No}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The subfield already knows.} Three verified papers across 2023--2026 cover both
directions; one is three months old.
\item \textbf{Deployed outcome prediction cannot have this bug.} Litigation analytics predicts from
judge, court, party and case-type features --- it \emph{cannot} read the court's reasoning, because at
prediction time nobody has written it. The leakage is purely an artifact of retrospectively feeding
decided judgments to a model: a \textbf{benchmark-construction finding, not an industry one}.
\item \textbf{Scale limits the claims.} Every outcome-model CI spans about 0.11.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
What this is instead: a well-controlled workshop paper (NLLP at EMNLP, or JURIX) with two real
methodological contributions and a constructive positive result. A good outcome for a class project;
not a disruption.

\subsection{Five transferable lessons}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A control is worth more than a method.} The copy control killed a statute result
($0.447 \rightarrow$ an honest $0.173$); the popularity control validated a precedent result; the
base-rate control dissolved an entire error taxonomy; Benjamini--Hochberg stopped a pattern library
shipping; volume-matching stopped a non-existent leak being reported. In every case the control
changed the conclusion.
\item \textbf{An indirect argument that an objection is unlikely is not a measurement.} The
last-512 contamination was dismissed on two true-but-unresponsive grounds before being tested
directly. A detector can mark a boundary \emph{correctly} and still leave the preceding sentence.
\item \textbf{Distinguish ``no answer'' from ``the answer is nothing''.} This single confusion
produced two separate false findings.
\item \textbf{Assert your joins.} A pipeline that silently processes 12\% of its input is worse than
one that crashes.
\item \textbf{Elapsed time is not progress, and 0\% CPU is not a hang.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{What is not established, and open blockers}\label{sec:blockers}

\paragraph{B2 --- element burden metadata does not exist \spec{8.3}} The ontology requires every
element to record who bears the burden of proof, to what standard, and when it shifts, and supplies
this for \textbf{0 of 66}. Two models produced degenerate output (92\% and 87\% assigned to one
class; one standard never used at all, including where it unambiguously applies). A gate refuses to
save such annotations, so \textbf{feature group \code{E} is empty} and every result labelled
\code{F+P+E+\ldots} contains no \code{E}. The fix is a few hours of expert hand annotation
(\code{Data/gold/element\_burden\_sheet.csv}), not a larger model.

\paragraph{B3 --- the vocabulary cannot be frozen \spec{6.2}} \textbf{20.3\%} of 79,496 canonical
facts are out of vocabulary against a 5\% freeze threshold. Note §\ref{sec:discretisation} shows
improving coverage would not rescue the predictive result.

\paragraph{B4 --- no gold annotation set \spec{6.4}} The specification asks for $\geq$150 cases
annotated by a legal reviewer with agreement on a 30-case overlap. The \textbf{150 cases are
prepared} (\code{Data/gold/gold\_tasks.jsonl}) and an annotation tool exists
(\code{src/annotate/app.py}), but no reviewer has annotated them, so \textbf{extraction precision and
recall against gold cannot be computed}. The four proxies in use and what each does \emph{not}
establish: grounding rate 0.92 bounds fabrication but says nothing about recall; the legal-statement
filter bounds one precision failure mode; rules-vs-LLM agreement 87.0\% estimates outcome-label
noise, not fact quality; the representation ladder measures downstream utility but cannot attribute a
loss to a specific extraction error. \textbf{This is the single biggest gap for a reviewer, and it is
blocked on human time, not compute.}

\paragraph{B5 --- the ILDC replication (optional)} The licence request sits in a review queue
expected not to be processed. \code{src/eval/ildc\_ablation.py} is written, setback-armed and
verified end to end via \code{--smoke-test}, and runs the moment \code{Data/ildc/} is populated. It
is \textbf{no longer load-bearing}: §\ref{sec:scale} covers the same population at comparable scale.
What ILDC would still add is gold labels and \emph{its own} deletion boundary, so it could show that
\emph{their} specific cut leaves residue rather than that a cut \emph{like} theirs does. The module
does not route around the gate. Note the authors are \textbf{not} inactive --- Nigam published through
2026 and Modi is organising the JUST NLP 2025 workshop --- so a direct email beats the queue.

\paragraph{B6 --- S4's \emph{correctness} is unmeasured, and nothing was ever fine-tuned} Two gaps
opened by §\ref{sec:v2}. First, S4 is reliable (96.9\%) and almost never fabricates (0.62\%), but
whether its verdicts are \emph{right} needs human labels; \code{v2/gold\_elements.py} builds a
40-case, 214-judgement set from B4's prepared cases, blind and with clicked rather than typed quotes,
at a reviewer budget of about 94 minutes. Nothing is annotated yet. Second, and more serious for the
ceiling claim, \textbf{no model in this project was ever fine-tuned} --- everything is TF-IDF with
logistic regression plus zero-shot and few-shot LLM calls, while the work we compare against used
fine-tuned transformers. §\ref{sec:can78} sets out why that experiment is worth running and why it
cuts both ways.

\paragraph{Other open items}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{What the cue scrub cannot separate.} \code{w0\_scrubbed} (0.775) and
\code{w0\_cue\_free} (0.742) both stay above \code{masked}, so some telegraphing language escapes the
patterns. Whether that remainder is undetected leakage or genuine summative reasoning is unresolved;
the setback curve sidesteps the question rather than answering it.
\item \textbf{Human verification of outcome labels \spec{5.1}.} The check of 200 labels is
outstanding; the 562 \code{UNRESOLVED} conflicts are the highest-information place to start.
\item \textbf{The attribution ceiling} (§\ref{sec:extraction}). Cause unknown.
\item \textbf{Statistical power.} Every outcome-model CI spans about 0.11; the structured model and a
per-decade majority baseline cannot be separated at $n=881$.
\item \textbf{§8 is mis-specified for this corpus}, not merely unmet.
\item \textbf{Not started}: the demo dashboard; the team roster and the official proposal template.
\end{itemize}
