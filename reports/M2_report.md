## 2. Fact extraction (§6.1)

Prompt `extract_facts.v2`, model `llama4-scout-17b` (chosen by measurement — §3).

Full corpus: **79,496 facts from 4,581 cases**, median 13 per case.

| | share of proposals |
|---|---|
| kept | **84.7%** |
| discarded, quote not locatable in the excerpt | 10.1% |
| discarded as a statement of law or about an authority | 3.5% |
| deduplicated across chunk overlap | 1.7% |
| **dropped calls** | **0** |

### 2.1 Spans are computed, not requested

§6.1 requires a `source_span`. Models count characters badly and copy text well, so the model is
required to quote verbatim and `src/grounding.py::locate` computes the offsets. A fact whose quote
cannot be located is **discarded**, never stored with a null span — an unlocatable fact is
indistinguishable from an invented one.

`facts_qa.py` verifies this end to end: **2,227 of 2,227 sampled spans round-trip exactly** against
`masked_text` under the grounding normalisation.

Grounding tolerates two measured extraction artifacts, applied to both sides so it can never
manufacture a match: mid-sentence line breaks, and pre-1980 hyphenated line wraps
(`plain- tiff's`) which a model silently repairs when quoting.

### 2.2 Party orientation: the fix was reading the right field

§8.1 builds `T_plaintiff` from facts asserted by the plaintiff **or admitted**, and `T_defendant`
likewise. A fact marked `court_narrative` enters neither view and is effectively discarded.

`asserted_by` came out **84% `court_narrative`**. Tightening the prompt and supplying the case
caption moved it only to 79.5%, and `asserted_by="admitted"` stayed at **0%** — while
`disputed_status="admitted"` was **81%**. The model uses one field for attribution and the other for
admittedness, which is the more natural reading of two overlapping fields.

Orientation now reads both. **Usable in a party view: 17% → 96%. Facts entering neither view:
79.5% → 4.0%.** The information was present throughout; no additional prompt engineering was needed.

### 2.3 Output is era-dependent (a confound, not a defect)

| decade | facts/case | attributed to a party | admitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1960s | 13.9 | **0.13** | 0.86 |
| 1970s | 13.6 | 0.19 | 0.79 |
| 1990s | 14.4 | 0.17 | 0.80 |
| 2010s | 24.8 | 0.24 | 0.77 |
| 2020s | **28.5** | **0.28** | 0.75 |

Modern judgments yield twice the facts and attribute them twice as often. This is how courts now
write. It is the **third confound** in §5.3's temporal split, after the 39%→64% outcome base-rate
drift and the HEADNOTE shift from 79.5% to 0.0%; temporal results must be read against the
era-balanced forum-held-out split.

## 3. Choosing the extraction model (§3.3 says state the LLM's role; here it is a component)

49 models are available. Scored on 20 cases stratified 1950–2022, identical for every model, with
ground-truth-free metrics because §6.4's gold set does not exist:

| model | grounding | legal leak | attribution | facts/case | s/case |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `llama4-scout-17b` | 0.92 | 0.03 | 0.27 | 18.4 | **4.2** |
| `llama4-maverick-17b` | **0.93** | **0.01** | **0.41** | **23.5** | 28.7 |
| `qwen3-30b-a3b-instruct-2507` | 0.92 | 0.03 | 0.40 | 10.5 | 15.7 |
| `olmo3-32b-instruct` | excluded | | | | ~49s/call |
| `glm-5-3-flash` | excluded | | | | hangs on long prompts |
| `glm-5-3` | excluded | | | | ~47s/call |

**scout was chosen despite maverick winning every quality metric**, because canonicalisation, not
extraction, was the dominant loss (0.097 against 0.043 at n=881) and coverage was the binding
constraint. Coverage is binding, and maverick would take 18–36h against scout's
~1.5h. maverick's attribution edge is queued as a controlled subset test rather than assumed to
matter or assumed not to.

**Two screening traps worth recording:**
- A one-line probe put `qwen3-30b` at 0.4s and `glm-5-3-flash` at 2.3s; on real extraction they take
  15.7s/case and hang. Latency is dominated by **output length** (~2,500 tokens), so throughput must
  never be screened on a short prompt.
- 11 of 26 candidates appeared unable to follow a JSON schema. At `max_tokens=3000` **all 11
  worked** — reasoning models whose thinking consumed a 120-token budget. `qwen35-27b` spent 1,491
  output tokens on a two-field answer.

## 4. Canonicalisation (§6.2) — where §6 acceptance fails

13,919 facts labelled against the 345-label vocabulary, candidates retrieved by embedding
similarity (24 per fact) rather than dumping all 345 into every prompt.

| | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| mapped to the vocabulary | 63,370 | **79.7%** |
| **out of vocabulary** | 16,126 | **20.3%** |
| §6.2 freeze threshold | | 5% |

(79,496 facts over 4,581 cases. An earlier figure of 22.7% was measured on the first 795 cases; the
full-corpus rate is 20.3%.)

### 4.1 The NEW-rate flag could not be trusted

Of the 3,155 out-of-vocabulary labels, the model flagged only **75 (2.4%)** with the `NEW:` escape.
The other 97.6% were labels absent from the vocabulary that it had **not** flagged, caught only by
checking its answers against the vocabulary.

Had the flag been trusted, the measured rate would have read **0.5%** — comfortably under §6.2's 5%
threshold — and we would have frozen a vocabulary that cannot name a fifth of the facts, then spent
the project wondering why no pattern reached significance. Out-of-vocabulary is now decided by
checking, not by trusting.

### 4.2 This is the pipeline's binding constraint

The representation ladder localises the loss precisely (see `reports/M5_report.md`):
masked text **0.657** → extracted fact text **0.614** → canonical atoms **0.517** (chance 0.500),
at n=881.

Extraction loses 0.043 and canonicalisation a further 0.097. An earlier reading of this report said
extraction was *lossless* (0.578 → 0.577); that held at n=143 and does not at n=881.

The decisive follow-up is in `reports/M5_report.md` §2: an **induced** vocabulary of equal
granularity loses just as much, and more atoms do not help. The problem is **discretisation**, not
this ontology's particular labels.
